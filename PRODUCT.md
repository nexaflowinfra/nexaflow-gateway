# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

NexaFlow Enquiry is for Singapore and Malaysia merchants, owners, and small sales teams who receive customer enquiries from scattered channels such as WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Xiaohongshu, calls, referrals, and enquiry links.

These users are busy operators, not software power users. They need to open one place, see who needs attention first, understand what the customer wants, and know what to say next without learning a complex CRM. Used car dealers are an early pilot segment, but the product positioning must stay broader than one industry.

## Product Purpose

NexaFlow Enquiry collects scattered customer enquiries into a private sales inbox. It helps merchants see the source, customer request, missing details, likely stuck point, urgency, suggested next reply, and follow-up timing.

Success means a merchant can understand the workflow in seconds: messages come in, NexaFlow organizes the buyer/customer queue, AI highlights what is missing or blocking conversion, and the salesperson follows up in the right channel with a clear next message.

## Brand Personality

Clear, premium, practical.

The brand should feel calm, trustworthy, technical, and business-focused. It should communicate that NexaFlow is a serious AI business operating system that can grow beyond enquiry handling into CRM, billing, inventory, automation, and dashboard products.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product feel like a generic AI SaaS template, a purple-blue gradient landing page, a decorative card wall, or a complex enterprise CRM.

Do not position the public homepage as only for used car dealers. Do not make "one-click reply" the whole value proposition, because merchants still need personal-skill follow-up, qualification, budget checking, loan/document discussion, comparison handling, and reminders.

Do not overload busy merchants with setup complexity, platform jargon, or too many visible controls. Advanced social integration, Meta setup, webhooks, and security details should be available when needed but not dominate the first impression.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the daily workflow, not a feature list.
2. Show who to reply first, what the customer needs, what is missing, and what to send next.
3. Keep the homepage broad for merchants while letting vertical demos, such as used car dealers, prove the value.
4. Make trust, privacy, and data protection visible without making the product feel heavy.
5. Keep every screen simple enough for a busy salesperson to use on mobile.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The interface should be bilingual in English and Chinese where customer-facing adoption depends on it. It should be mobile-first, high contrast, readable in bright environments, and usable with touch targets that do not crowd or overlap.

Motion should be restrained and should respect reduced-motion preferences. Text should never overflow buttons, cards, or mobile containers. Color should not be the only way to communicate status or priority.
